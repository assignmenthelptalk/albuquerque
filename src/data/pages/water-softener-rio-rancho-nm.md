## Why Rio Rancho Residents Need a Water Softener

Rio Rancho water is groundwater, and groundwater carries the minerals of the rock it moves through. The City's 2025 Consumer Confidence Report says the aquifer lies within volcanic rock and that many of the City's wells are over 1,000 feet deep. The report states no hardness figure, so the number for your home comes from a tap test. <mark class="data-gap">[NEEDS DATA: Rio Rancho's measured hardness from the City's Utilities Department.]</mark>

The City occasionally buys water from the Albuquerque Bernalillo County Water Utility Authority to cover maintenance: about 2 million gallons a day for about a month in May and June 2025. ABCWUA's 2023 report put the Albuquerque average at 8.44 grains per gallon, and that figure is not assumed to be Rio Rancho's. If a tap test reads 8 gpg, a household of four using 300 gallons a day removes about 2,400 grains daily, so a 32,000-grain salt-based softener regenerates about every 13 days. If it reads 15 gpg, the same household removes about 4,500 grains daily, and a 48,000-grain unit regenerates about every 11 days.

## About Rio Rancho Water Quality

The City's 2025 report found arsenic at 4 to 5.8 parts per billion, against a limit of 10 ppb, and says the arsenic is naturally occurring in the volcanic rock. A softener does not remove arsenic, so ask about [reverse osmosis](/reverse-osmosis-installation/) if you want it lower at the drinking tap. About 2,500 Rio Rancho homes were on private domestic wells according to a 2004 City plan reported by the Rio Rancho Observer, and the City's results do not cover them, so a private well needs its own test.
