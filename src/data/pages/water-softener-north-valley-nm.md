## Why North Valley Residents Need a Water Softener

Some North Valley households use private wells, and a well is not covered by any utility report. A 2017 University of New Mexico water resources report titled “Middle Rio Grande Surface and Well Water Quality and the Health Implications to Humans” analyzes chemical and microbiological well-water testing in the North Valley, South Valley and Albuquerque Acres, and its abstract notes that homes on private wells typically handle wastewater with septic and leaching systems. A well test, not a city report, tells you what your water carries.

The North Valley sits in the Rio Grande valley, and a 1988 U.S. Geological Survey report by Scott K. Anderholm found that ground water there is affected by the infiltration of excess irrigation water, which generally has a larger specific conductance than other ground water in the valley, so shallow ground water generally has a larger specific conductance than deeper ground water. Specific conductance measures dissolved minerals, which is why two wells in the same neighborhood can read differently.

## About North Valley Water Quality

For homes on the city system, ABCWUA blends locally pumped groundwater with treated San Juan-Chama surface water, and its 2023 report gave a city range of 2.09 to 11.72 grains per gallon with a city average of 8.44. ABCWUA treats and tests that water before it reaches homes. Private wells are not tested for you, and well-water guidance recommends testing for bacteria every year, with nitrate and other substances on a schedule that depends on local conditions.
