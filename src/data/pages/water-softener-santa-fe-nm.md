## Why Santa Fe Residents Need a Water Softener

Santa Fe water is a blend, so the hardness at a tap depends on which sources serve the street that day. The City's 2024 water quality report says the system draws on the Santa Fe River through the McClure and Nichols reservoirs and the Canyon Road plant, on the Rio Grande through the Buckman Regional Water Treatment Plant, which began delivering water in 2011, on a City Well Field of 6 active wells inside the city limits, and on the Buckman Well Field of 13 wells about 15 miles northwest. All four sources are treated with chlorine, and the report says the water met every EPA and state limit in 2024.

The 2024 report does not state a hardness figure. The City's 2008 water quality table listed calcium and magnesium hardness of 131.89 to 226.58 mg/L for its City wells, about 7.7 to 13.2 grains per gallon, and its 2011 report says the added Rio Grande surface water lowered calcium and magnesium hardness. <mark class="data-gap">[NEEDS DATA: a current measured hardness for Santa Fe from the City's Water Division or a tap test.]</mark> If a tap test reads 13 gpg, a household of four using 300 gallons a day removes about 3,900 grains daily, so a 48,000-grain salt-based softener regenerates about every 12 days. If it reads 8 gpg, the same household removes about 2,400 grains daily, and the unit regenerates about every 20 days.

## About Santa Fe Water Quality

The City of Santa Fe publishes an annual water quality report, and the 2024 edition lists its regulated results by source. Because Santa Fe blends surface water and groundwater, the supply can change through the year, and your pipes change what reaches the tap. A softener does not remove contaminants such as arsenic, so ask about [water treatment and testing](/water-treatment-and-testing/) options if a test shows a concern. Homes outside the City's service area on private wells need their own test.
